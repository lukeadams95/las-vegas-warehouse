Types of Warehouse Storage Facilities Explained

September 22, 2026 · 4 min read

Warehouse storage facilities are not interchangeable. A building that works perfectly for pallets of packaged goods can be the wrong choice for wine, artwork, or anything sensitive to heat. Picking the wrong type usually shows up later as damaged inventory or a bill for services you are not using. This guide breaks down the main facility types and what each one is actually built for, drawn from more than 20 years of operating a full service warehouse in Las Vegas.

The Four Main Types of Warehouse Storage Facilities

Most facilities fall into one of four categories. Some operations, including ours, combine several under one roof.

Public Warehouse Storage

Shared space rented to multiple businesses, usually billed by pallet or square footage. You get professional handling without leasing an entire building or hiring your own crew. This is the most common fit for small and mid sized businesses, seasonal overflow, and companies testing a new market.

Private and Contract Warehousing

A dedicated arrangement where space and staff are committed to one client, typically under a longer agreement. It costs more but offers more control, and it suits businesses with steady, predictable volume. Our warehouse services page outlines where each arrangement makes sense.

Bonded Warehousing

A facility authorized to store imported goods before duties are paid. Importers use it to defer costs until items are ready to enter the market. If you are not importing, you do not need it.

Specialty and Climate Controlled Facilities

Built for goods that standard storage would damage. Temperature and humidity are held steady and access is tightly documented. This is the category behind our secure art storage and our dedicated wine and whiskey storage, where a few degrees of drift over a summer can cost a collection real value.

How to Match a Facility Type to What You Store

Work backward from your goods rather than from price. Three questions get you most of the way there.

Are Your Goods Sensitive to Temperature or Humidity?

Wine, spirits, fine art, antiques, electronics, and paper records all degrade in unstable conditions. In a desert climate this is not a minor detail. If any of these describe your inventory, climate control is the requirement, not the upgrade.

How Fast Do Your Goods Move?

Inventory that sits for months needs different handling than freight that turns over in days. For fast moving shipments, ask whether the facility offers cross docking so goods can transfer between trucks without long term storage costs.

What Level of Security and Documentation Do You Need?

Standard inventory needs monitored access. High value goods need logged entries and a documented chain of custody. Fire protection matters too, and it varies by what is stored and how high it is stacked. NFPA guidance on warehouse fire protection explains why sprinkler design changes with commodity type and rack configuration, which is a fair question to ask any facility you tour.

Questions to Ask Before You Choose a Facility

Bring these to every provider on your shortlist.

  • Which facility types do you actually operate, and which are subcontracted?
  • How is climate controlled space monitored, and what happens if conditions drift?
  • How am I billed, by pallet, by square footage, or by service?
  • Can I move between storage types as my needs change?

For an independent reference point, the International Warehouse Logistics Association maintains a directory of vetted third party warehouse providers and the standards its members are expected to document.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the main types of warehouse storage facilities?

Public, private or contract, bonded, and specialty facilities such as climate controlled and high security storage.

What is public warehouse storage?

Shared space rented out to multiple businesses, typically billed by pallet or square footage, with staff handling receiving and inventory.

Do I need a specialized facility for fragile or high value goods?

Yes. Items like art, wine, and electronics need climate control and tighter security than standard bulk storage provides.

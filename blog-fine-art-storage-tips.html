5 Tips for Safely Storing and Shipping Fine Art

August 19, 2026 · 4 min read

Fine art doesn't forgive careless handling. A single mistake in transit or storage can permanently devalue a piece that took years — or decades — to create. Whether you're a gallery, collector, or institution, these five fundamentals go a long way toward keeping your collection intact.

1. Control the Climate

Temperature and humidity swings are one of the biggest threats to paintings, sculptures, and mixed-media works. Wood warps, canvas stretches, and pigments crack under fluctuating conditions. Look for storage that maintains consistent, climate-considered environments rather than a standard warehouse setting.

2. Invest in Custom Crating

Off-the-shelf boxes aren't built for irregularly shaped statues, framed canvases, or fragile antiques. Custom crates, built to the exact dimensions and weight of each piece, absorb shock and prevent shifting during transport — especially critical for cross-country or international shipments.

3. Document Condition Before and After

Photograph and log the condition of every piece before it leaves your hands and again when it arrives at its destination. A clear paper trail protects you and your storage or shipping partner if a dispute ever arises.

4. Limit Handling

Every touchpoint is a risk. Work with a team trained specifically in fine art handling — not general freight staff — and minimize the number of times a piece is moved, unpacked, or repositioned between pickup and final placement.

5. Choose a Partner With a Real Track Record

Ask about experience with museum exhibits, private collections, and public installations. A storage and logistics partner who has handled sculptures, statues, and gallery pieces at scale will anticipate problems a general mover won't even think to look for.
